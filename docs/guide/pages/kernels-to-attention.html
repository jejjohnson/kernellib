Attention is a kernel smoother that never solves

A cloud mask has punched holes in a day of TROPOMI methane. Kriging, Nadaraya–Watson and one attention head each fill a hole with a weighted sum of the cells they can see. Written as operators with fixed signatures, they show exactly what attention keeps from kriging and what it drops.

TL;DR

Four operators, one gap

  • Kriging gets its weights from a solve, \(\vect{w}_* = (\mat{K}_{SS} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \mat{I})^{-1} \mat{K}_{*S}^\top\). The solve declusters redundant cells, so weights can be negative and need not sum to one.
  • Nadaraya–Watson drops the solve and normalises the kernel row instead: weights are non-negative and sum to one. With an RBF kernel on equal-norm inputs it is exactly a softmax of dot products.
  • An attention head is Nadaraya–Watson on learned features, \(P_{mn} = \op{softmax}_n(\vect{q}_m^\top \vect{k}_n / \sqrt{D_q})\). Its kernel is asymmetric and indefinite, a smoothing kernel rather than a Mercer kernel.
  • Tile tokens, a ViT's "patches", make the kernel act between regions, which is learned non-local means. Multi-scale behaviour comes from heads with different lengthscales \(\ell_h\), from depth and from hierarchy, not from the softmax.
  • The test: under the plume cloud the truth is 1,930.0 ppb. Kriging gives 1,924.0 ± 7.8 ppb, Nadaraya–Watson 1,915.9 ppb, and the 20 km head 1,922.3 ppb. Only kriging says how far to trust its number.

1 · Kriging solves for its weights

Under the plume cloud there is no measurement, only 763 clear cells around it. Kriging treats the XCH₄ field as a draw from a Gaussian process, conditions on those cells, and reports the posterior mean and variance at the query. The weights it puts on each cell come out of a linear solve against the Gram matrix of the observed cells. That solve is the step every later operator on this page drops.

Model

Kriging is the GP posterior mean under four assumptions

1Joint

Everything in the scene: the observations \(\vect{y}\), the latent field at the observed cells \(\vect{f}\), and the value we want under the cloud \(f_*\), with the hyperparameters fixed after the semicolon.

\[ p(\vect{y}, \vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) = p(\vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) \]
2Factorisationuses A1

Each retrieval depends only on the field at its own cell, so the likelihood splits per cell and does not involve \(f_*\):

\[ p(\vect{y}, \vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) = p(\vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) \prod_{n=1}^{N_{\Omega_y}} p(y_n \mid f_n; \gvect{\theta}) \]
3Objective

The prediction is the mode of the posterior of \(f_*\). Integrate out \(\vect{f}\) and write it as a cost to minimise:

\[ \mathcal{J}(f_*) = -\log \int p(\vect{y}, \vect{f}, f_*; \gvect{\theta}) \mathrm{d}\vect{f} \] \[ \hat{f}_* = \mathrm{argmin}_{f_*} \mathcal{J}(f_*) \]

Nothing so far is Gaussian. The assumptions say what makes the integral closed-form.

4Assumptions
A1
Independent Gaussian noise. \[ p(y_n \mid f_n; \gvect{\theta}) = \mathcal{N}(y_n; f_n, \sigma_\varepsilon^2) \] Breaks when retrieval errors are correlated along track (albedo, aerosol); a full \(\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon\) fixes it.
A2
Gaussian-process prior. \[ f \sim \mathcal{GP}(\mu_f, k) \] Breaks for plumes: XCH₄ has a heavy right tail and the plume is a non-stationary feature. Warped or non-stationary GPs fix it.
A3
Stationary RBF kernel. \[ k(\vect{s}, \vect{s}') = \sigma_k^2 \exp(-\frac{\Vert \vect{s} - \vect{s}' \Vert^2}{2 \ell^2}) \] Breaks at plume edges, which are sharper than any single \(\ell\) allows; a Matérn or non-stationary kernel helps.
A4
Plug-in mean. Replace \(\mu_f\) by the scene mean \(\bar{y}\) of the observed cells.This is simple kriging. Ordinary kriging estimates the mean jointly by forcing \(\sum_n w_{*n} = 1\).
5Resultuses A1–A4

The posterior of \(f_*\) is Gaussian, so its mode is its mean:

\[ \hat{f}_* = \bar{y} + \mat{K}_{*S} (\mat{K}_{SS} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \mat{I})^{-1} (\vect{y} - \bar{y} \vect{1}) \] \[ \sigma_*^2 = \sigma_k^2 - \mat{K}_{*S} (\mat{K}_{SS} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \mat{I})^{-1} \mat{K}_{*S}^\top \]

Naming \(\vect{w}_* = (\mat{K}_{SS} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \mat{I})^{-1} \mat{K}_{*S}^\top\) gives lines 4 and 5 of the GP box.

Watch out

Simple kriging weights need not sum to one: under the plume cloud \(\sum_n w_{*n} = 0.78\), so 22% of the local anomaly is pulled back towards \(\bar{y} = 1{,}910.9\) ppb. Far from any clear cell the prediction reverts to the scene mean, not to the nearest observation.

Running example · kriging

At \(\vect{s}_* = (77.5, 77.5)\) km the solve concentrates weight on the ring of clear cells at the cloud edge, with the smallest weight −0.23. The prediction is 1,924.0 ± 7.8 ppb against a truth of 1,930.0 ppb.

2 · Nadaraya–Watson normalises instead

Drop the Gram matrix and keep only the kernel row between the query and each clear cell. Divide by its sum and you have weights that are non-negative and sum to one: a local weighted average. That is the Nadaraya–Watson smoother. It has its own probabilistic reading, as a conditional mean under a kernel density estimate, and that reading shows exactly which assumption replaced the solve.

Model

Nadaraya–Watson is a conditional mean under a kernel density estimate

1Joint

The joint density of a cell's location and its XCH₄, which we never write down directly; we only have the clear-cell pairs \((\vect{s}_n, y_n)\).

\[ p(\vect{s}, y) \]
2Factorisation

Condition on the query location:

\[ p(y \mid \vect{s}_*) = \frac{p(\vect{s}_*, y)}{\int p(\vect{s}_*, y') \mathrm{d}y'} \]
3Objective

Under squared loss the best single number is the conditional mean:

\[ \mathcal{J}(c) = \int (y - c)^2 p(y \mid \vect{s}_*) \mathrm{d}y \] \[ \hat{f}_* = \mathrm{argmin}_c \mathcal{J}(c) = \mathbb{E}[y \mid \vect{s}_*] \]
4Assumptions
A1
Independent pairs. The \((\vect{s}_n, y_n)\) are i.i.d. draws from \(p(\vect{s}, y)\).Breaks for any spatial field: neighbouring cells are correlated, which is exactly what kriging's solve accounts for. NW therefore double-counts clustered cells.
A2
Product kernel density estimate. \[ \hat{p}(\vect{s}, y) = \frac{1}{N_{\Omega_y}} \sum_{n=1}^{N_{\Omega_y}} \kappa_\ell(\vect{s} - \vect{s}_n) \kappa_b(y - y_n) \] Breaks when \(N_{\Omega_y}\) is small relative to the dimension of \(\vect{s}\), the curse of dimensionality.
A3
Symmetric \(y\)-kernel. \[ \int y \kappa_b(y - y_n) \mathrm{d}y = y_n \] Holds for any symmetric \(\kappa_b\) that integrates to one; its bandwidth \(b\) then drops out.
A4
RBF space kernel. \[ \kappa_\ell(\Delta \vect{s}) \propto \exp(-\frac{\Vert \Delta \vect{s} \Vert^2}{2 \ell^2}) \] The normalising constant cancels in the ratio, so only the shape matters.
5Resultuses A1–A4
\[ \hat{f}_* = \frac{\sum_{n} \kappa_\ell(\vect{s}_* - \vect{s}_n) y_n}{\sum_{n'} \kappa_\ell(\vect{s}_* - \vect{s}_{n'})} \]

Writing the kernel as \(\exp(a_{*n})\) gives lines 1–3 of the NW box.

Concept

An RBF kernel is already a softmax

Expand the exponent of the RBF:

\[ -\frac{\Vert \vect{s}_* - \vect{s}_n \Vert^2}{2 \ell^2} = \frac{\vect{s}_*^\top \vect{s}_n}{\ell^2} - \frac{\Vert \vect{s}_* \Vert^2}{2 \ell^2} - \frac{\Vert \vect{s}_n \Vert^2}{2 \ell^2} \]

The \(\Vert \vect{s}_* \Vert^2\) term is the same for every \(n\) and cancels in the normalisation. If every \(\Vert \vect{s}_n \Vert\) is equal too, the NW weights are exactly a softmax of dot products:

\[ w_{*n} = \op{softmax}_n(\frac{\vect{s}_*^\top \vect{s}_n}{\ell^2}) \]

On Earth this holds exactly. Take the coordinates as unit vectors \(\vect{s}_n \in S^2\). Then \(\Vert \vect{s}_n - \vect{s}_{n'} \Vert^2 = 2 - 2 \vect{s}_n^\top \vect{s}_{n'}\), and an RBF on chord distance is the von Mises–Fisher kernel \(\exp(\vect{s}_n^\top \vect{s}_{n'} / \ell^2)\). In a transformer, LayerNorm holds token norms nearly constant, so the dot-product logit behaves like a negative squared distance, and \(\sqrt{D_q}\) plays the part of \(\ell^2\).

3 · An attention head is Nadaraya–Watson on learned features

Give every cell a token: a feature vector \(\vect{z}_n\) that could hold the retrieved XCH₄, SWIR albedo, aerosol optical depth, surface pressure and a position encoding. Cloud-flagged cells get tokens too, built from what surrounds them, so they can ask questions even though they cannot answer them. An attention head maps each token to a query, a key and a value, scores every query against every key, and returns a softmax-weighted average of values. That is the NW box with \(\vect{s}\) replaced by learned features and a dot product in place of the distance.

Concept

The attention weight is a bilinear-exponential kernel

Substitute lines 1–2 of the A box into line 4. The logit is a bilinear form in the raw features:

\[ a_{mn} = \frac{\vect{z}_m^\top \mat{A} \vect{z}_n}{\sqrt{D_q}} \] \[ \mat{A} = \mat{W}_Q \mat{W}_K^\top \] \[ P_{mn} = \frac{o_n \exp(a_{mn})}{\sum_{n'} o_{n'} \exp(a_{mn'})} \]
  • \(\mat{A}\) has rank at most \(D_q\) and is in general neither symmetric nor positive semi-definite. So \(\exp(a_{mn})\) is a smoothing kernel in the NW sense, a non-negative weight, but not a Mercer kernel with an RKHS.
  • Tying \(\mat{W}_Q = \mat{W}_K\) makes \(\mat{A}\) positive semi-definite; the exponential of a positive-definite kernel is positive definite, so the tied head is a Mercer kernel.
  • The asymmetry is useful. Whom a downwind plume cell should listen to (its source) is not who should listen to it.
  • \(\mat{P}\) is row-stochastic: it is the NW hat matrix, the counterpart of the GP's \(\mat{K}_{SS} (\mat{K}_{SS} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \mat{I})^{-1}\).
  • When \(\vect{z}\) mixes position and content, the weight factors roughly into a spatial term times a content term. That is a bilateral filter: near and similar.
Where \(\mat{A}\)the head's metric on feature space, rank \(\le D_q\)\(\mathbb{R}^{D_z \times D_z}\)— \(D_z, D_q\)token width and head width\(\mathbb{N}\)— \(o_n\)1 if cell \(n\) is clear, so only clear cells serve as keys\(\{0, 1\}\)bool \(\mat{P}\)attention matrix, each row a weight vector like \(\vect{w}_*\)\(\mathbb{R}^{N_{\Omega_x} \times N_{\Omega_x}}\)—

The general statement is that a head scores each pair of tokens with some function of their features and positions, exponentiates and normalises. The lab cannot train a network, so it uses a head built by hand from three interpretable parts, which is the simple instance below. The general instance learns \(\mat{A}\).

Simple instance · the lab head \[ a_{mn} = \gamma c_m c_n - \frac{\Vert \vect{s}_m - \vect{s}_n \Vert}{\ell_h} + \beta \frac{(\vect{s}_m - \vect{s}_n)^\top \vect{u}}{\Vert \vect{u} \Vert L_\beta} \] \[ c_n = \frac{\bar{y}^{(p_n)} - \bar{y}}{\hat{\sigma}_y} \]

The content term is a rank-one \(\mat{A}\): plume tokens pull towards plume tokens. Exponentiated, \(-\Vert \Delta \vect{s} \Vert / \ell_h\) is a Matérn-½ kernel. The β term rewards keys upwind of the query; it is antisymmetric in \(m, n\), so \(P_{mn} \ne P_{nm}\). Values are \(v_n = y_n\), in ppb.

General instance · a trained head \[ a_{mn} = \frac{\vect{z}_m^\top \mat{W}_Q \mat{W}_K^\top \vect{z}_n}{\sqrt{D_q}} \]

\(\mat{W}_Q\), \(\mat{W}_K\) and \(\mat{W}_V\) are learned from many scenes (Train card below). Position enters through \(\vect{z}\) or through a bias added to \(a_{mn}\); the lab's three terms are one point in this family.

Here \(\bar{y}^{(p_n)}\) is the mean of the clear cells in patch \(\Omega_x^{(p_n)}\), the smallest square window around cell \(n\) that holds at least three of them. Patches overlap, so a token under a cloud still carries what its cloud edge saw. \(\hat{\sigma}_y = 6.3\) ppb is the sample standard deviation of the clear cells, and \(L_\beta = 20\) km is a fixed length.

Code sketch: three smoothers side by side

Every function here is defined on this page, so each is marked # user. Read nadaraya_watson and attention_head line by line: only the logit and the values change. kriging_mean has the one line attention never has, the solve.

# Shapes: N_Ωy observed cells (obs); N_Ωx = 1,024 cells = tokens (token);
#         D_z token width (feat); D_q head width (head_feat)
# Units:  [y] = ppb;  [s] = km;  [1] = dimensionless
import einx
import jax.numpy as jnp
import lineax as lx
from jaxtyping import Array, Bool, Float

# user
def sq_dist(
    a: Float[Array, "A 2"], b: Float[Array, "B 2"]
) -> Float[Array, "A B"]:
    # Δs_ij = a_i − b_j
    delta = einx.subtract("a c, b c -> a b c", a, b)          # → (A, B, 2)      [s]
    # ‖Δs_ij‖²
    return einx.sum("a b [c]", delta**2)                        # → (A, B)         [s]²


# user
def kriging_mean(s_query, s_obs, y_obs, ell, sigma_k, sigma_eps):
    # ȳ = (1/N_Ωy) ∑ₙ yₙ
    y_bar = einx.mean("[obs]", y_obs)                           # (obs,) → ()     [y]
    # [K_SS]ₙₙ' = σₖ² exp(−‖sₙ − sₙ'‖² / 2ℓ²)
    gram = sigma_k**2 * jnp.exp(-sq_dist(s_obs, s_obs) / (2 * ell**2))   # (obs, obs)  [y]²
    # K_SS + σ_ε² I
    gram = gram + sigma_eps**2 * jnp.eye(gram.shape[0])         # (obs, obs) PSD   [y]²
    # [K_*S]ₙ = σₖ² exp(−‖s_* − sₙ‖² / 2ℓ²)
    cross = sigma_k**2 * jnp.exp(-sq_dist(s_query, s_obs) / (2 * ell**2))  # (Q, obs)  [y]²
    # not pinned (lineax)
    # α = (K_SS + σ_ε² I)⁻¹ (y − ȳ 1)
    op = lx.MatrixLinearOperator(gram, lx.positive_semidefinite_tag)
    alpha = lx.linear_solve(op, y_obs - y_bar, solver=lx.Cholesky()).value  # (obs,)  [y]⁻¹
    # f̂_* = ȳ + K_*S α  (= ȳ + w_*ᵀ(y − ȳ 1))
    return y_bar + einx.dot("q obs, obs -> q", cross, alpha)   # → (Q,)           [y]


# user
def nadaraya_watson(s_query, s_obs, y_obs, ell):
    # a_*ₙ = −‖s_* − sₙ‖² / 2ℓ²
    logits = -sq_dist(s_query, s_obs) / (2 * ell**2)            # → (Q, obs)       [1]
    # w_*ₙ = softmaxₙ(a_*ₙ)
    weights = einx.softmax("q [obs]", logits)                   # (Q, obs) rows → 1 [1]
    # f̂_* = ∑ₙ w_*ₙ yₙ
    return einx.dot("q obs, obs -> q", weights, y_obs)         # → (Q,)           [y]


# user
def attention_head(
    z: Float[Array, "token feat"],
    observed: Bool[Array, "token"],
    w_q: Float[Array, "feat head_feat"],
    w_k: Float[Array, "feat head_feat"],
    w_v: Float[Array, "feat head_feat"],
) -> Float[Array, "token head_feat"]:
    # q_m = W_Qᵀ z_m
    q = einx.dot("t f, f d -> t d", z, w_q)                    # → (token, head_feat) [1]
    # k_n = W_Kᵀ z_n
    k = einx.dot("t f, f d -> t d", z, w_k)                    # → (token, head_feat) [1]
    # v_n = W_Vᵀ z_n
    v = einx.dot("t f, f d -> t d", z, w_v)                    # → (token, head_feat) [v]
    # a_mn = q_mᵀ k_n / √D_q
    logits = einx.dot("m d, n d -> m n", q, k) / jnp.sqrt(q.shape[-1])  # (token, token) [1]
    # a_mn = −∞ where o_n = 0 (cloudy keys)
    logits = einx.where("n, m n, ", observed, logits, -jnp.inf) # (token, token)   [1]
    # P_mn = softmaxₙ(a_mn)
    weights = einx.softmax("m [n]", logits)                     # (token, token) rows → 1 [1]
    # v̄_m = ∑ₙ P_mn v_n
    return einx.dot("m n, n d -> m d", weights, v)             # → (token, head_feat) [v]

The same two smoothers in kernellib

The sketch above writes the Gram matrix and the solve by hand so every line maps to an equation. In kernellib the kernel is an object, to_operator adds the noise and tags the operator PSD, and gaussx picks the solve. This block runs as written and matches the dense formulas to 10⁻¹¹ ppb.

# Shapes: N_Ωy observed cells (obs); Q query cells (q)
# Units:  [y] = ppb;  [s] = km;  [1] = dimensionless
import einx
import gaussx as gx
import kernellib as kl

# today (kernellib)
# k(s, s') = σₖ² exp(−‖s − s'‖² / 2ℓ²),  ℓ = 15 km, σₖ = 12 ppb
kernel = kl.RBF(lengthscale=15.0, variance=12.0**2)
# ȳ = (1/N_Ωy) ∑ₙ yₙ
y_bar = einx.mean("[obs]", y_obs)                          # (obs,) → ()        [y]
# today (kernellib)
# K_SS + σ_ε² I,  σ_ε = 2.5 ppb
op = kl.to_operator(kernel, s_obs, noise=2.5**2)            # (obs, 2) → (obs, obs) PSD op [y]²
# today (gaussx)
# α = (K_SS + σ_ε² I)⁻¹ (y − ȳ 1)
alpha = gx.solve(op, y_obs - y_bar)                         # → (obs,)          [y]⁻¹
# today (kernellib)
# [K_*S]_qn = k(s_q, sₙ)
cross = kernel(s_query, s_obs)                              # → (Q, obs)        [y]²
# f̂_q = ȳ + K_*S α                                     (kriging)
f_kriging = y_bar + einx.dot("q obs, obs -> q", cross, alpha)   # → (Q,)     [y]
# w_qn = k(s_q, sₙ) / ∑ₙ' k(s_q, sₙ')                    (no solve)
weights = einx.divide("q obs, q -> q obs", cross, einx.sum("q [obs]", cross))  # (Q, obs) [1]
# f̂_q = ∑ₙ w_qn yₙ                                     (Nadaraya–Watson)
f_nw = einx.dot("q obs, obs -> q", weights, y_obs)       # → (Q,)           [y]

Watch out

\(\mat{P}\) is not a posterior. One head has no notion of redundancy, so two duplicate keys get twice the weight, and it returns no \(\sigma_*\). A single layer cannot produce kriging's negative weights; stacks of layers can approximate the solve (von Oswald et al., 2023), but the uncertainty does not come back.

The 20 km head at the plume query gives 1,912.3 ppb with γ = 0, 1,922.3 ppb at γ = 1.5 and 1,933.1 ppb at γ = 3. 4 · Tiles make it a kernel between regions; heads make it multi-scale

A vision transformer does not make one token per cell. It cuts the scene into square tiles of \(P \times P\) cells, flattens each tile and embeds it, so a token stands for a region. In the notation here those are tiles \(\Omega_x^{(r)}\), not patches, because they cover the scene without overlap; the lab's content windows overlap, so they are patches. With tile tokens the logit is a bilinear form on tile vectors, which makes tile attention a learned version of non-local means (Buades, Coll and Morel, 2005): weights from region similarity, values from the regions.

Each head is its own Nadaraya–Watson smoother with its own bandwidth \(\ell_h\). Line 5 sums smoothers, which is not a smoother with a summed kernel, because each head normalises separately.

Multi-scale behaviour enters in four places, each a familiar kernel construction:

  • Heads. The bias \(-\Vert \Delta \vect{s} \Vert / \ell_h\) makes each head's spatial factor a Matérn-½ kernel. A geometric ladder of \(\ell_h\) is a bank of exponential kernels, the 2-D analogue of ALiBi's slopes \(2^{-h}\) (Press et al., 2022).
  • Position bias times content. \(\exp(\text{content} + \text{bias})\) is a product kernel, so each head is local in space and selective in content at the same time.
  • Depth. Layer \(l + 1\) smooths features already smoothed by layer \(l\). That is kernel composition, as in deep kernel learning and deep GPs, and the receptive field grows with depth.
  • Hierarchy. Window attention plus 2 × 2 tile merging (Swin) builds an explicit resolution pyramid, like multigrid.
